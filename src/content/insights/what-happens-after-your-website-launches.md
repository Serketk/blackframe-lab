Launch day gets most of the attention in a website project. It's the visible milestone — the thing that gets shared, the thing that marks the project as "done." But a website's life doesn't end there. In most cases, it's just starting.

## The site keeps needing decisions

Once a website is live, it keeps generating small decisions that nobody accounted for during the build: a new service needs a page, a blog post needs publishing, a form stops forwarding correctly, an image needs replacing, analytics need reviewing. None of these are dramatic on their own. Left unattended, they add up to a site that quietly drifts out of date.

## Analytics only matter if someone looks at them

Most websites launch with analytics installed and never checked again. Google Analytics, Search Console and tag management are only useful if someone is actually reviewing what they show — what pages people land on, where they leave, what's technically broken. Without that review, the data collects but nothing changes because of it.

## Content is the part that ages fastest

Design ages slowly. Content ages constantly. Services change, team members change, pricing changes, new work gets completed. A website that isn't updated to reflect the current state of the business starts working against it — visitors make decisions based on information that's no longer accurate.

## Ownership matters more than any single fix

The businesses that keep a website working well after launch usually have one thing in common: someone is responsible for it. Not necessarily full-time, but clearly accountable — for publishing, for technical health, for noticing when something needs attention before a visitor does.

A launched website is a starting point. What happens in the months after determines whether it keeps earning its place.
