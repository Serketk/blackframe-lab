"Our website needs a redesign" is one of the most common requests a studio hears, and it's often the wrong diagnosis. Sometimes a website needs to be rebuilt. Sometimes it needs a handful of targeted fixes. Knowing which one you're dealing with saves time and budget.

## Signs that point to a redesign

A full redesign is usually the right call when the problems are structural rather than cosmetic:

- The information architecture no longer matches the business — new services, new audiences, or new priorities don't fit the existing structure.
- The site is difficult or risky to update, and small changes require disproportionate effort.
- The visual system actively undersells the quality of the work or the seniority of the business behind it.
- The site was built on a platform or foundation that limits what the business needs it to do now.

## Signs that point to something smaller

Not every complaint about a website is actually a redesign problem:

- A handful of pages feel dated, but the overall structure still works.
- Analytics show a specific page underperforming, not the whole site.
- The site is fine, but content hasn't been updated in a long time.
- A single new offer or campaign needs its own page, not a rebuilt site.

These are usually addressed through targeted updates, new pages, or focused UX/UI improvements — not a ground-up rebuild.

## The audit comes before the decision

The most reliable way to tell which situation you're in is a structured audit: reviewing the current site's information architecture, content, technical health and UX before deciding on scope. Committing to a full redesign without that step risks solving the wrong problem, or solving the right problem at a much higher cost than necessary.

A redesign is a significant undertaking. It's worth being certain it's the one you actually need.
